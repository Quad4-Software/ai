# Genre shapes

Different genres have different human shapes. The same text can
read as machine in one genre and normal in another.

## Essays and blog posts

- Thesis emerges rather than announces. No "In this essay I will"
  scaffolding.
- Paragraph lengths track argument weight. Long where the argument
  is dense, one line where a point lands.
- Ending is payoff, not summary. Never "In conclusion".
- Personal stake and first person are normal. Digression tolerated
  in moderation.
- Lists are rare in classic essay form.
- The tells to kill: announce-then-execute structure and the
  "challenges and future prospects" closer.

## Academic papers

- Structure is genre-required (IMRaD), so self-announcing sections
  are partially normal. Machine shape shows in the abstract and
  introduction: uniform paragraph rhythm, excess-vocabulary style
  words, hedged claims without citations.
- Academic hedging attaches to evidence. Machine hedging floats
  free of it.
- Real papers hedge specific claims and cite. Machine text hedges
  everything and cites nothing, or invents citations. Fabricated
  references are a machine tell in this genre.
- Kill "these findings have important implications" conclusions
  that state no implication.

## Forum and comment replies

The anti-essay. Good human replies:

- Open on the specific thing being answered. Quote it or name it.
- 1-3 short paragraphs. First person.
- Personal experience as evidence: "I tried X in March and it
  broke because..."
- Direct disagreement on a specific point, concessions on
  sub-points.
- No headings, no intro or outro scaffolding, no closing summary.
- Informal register. Typos tolerated.
- Stack Overflow answer guidance distilled: answer the specific
  question, state assumptions, "do not do that" must come with
  "try this instead", brevity is fine.
- A comment reply with headings and a conclusion is almost
  definitionally slop regardless of the words used.

## Emails

- Subject line does work.
- Bottom line up front. One topic per email.
- Short paragraphs, specific asks with names and dates.
- Sign-off per relationship. No headers or bold-term lists except
  logistics threads.
- Hedges are social ("I might be wrong, but") not epistemic ("it
  is important to note").
- LLM imitation works best in email (constrained shape), so the
  remaining tells are subtler: generic filler, over-apology, recap
  padding.

## Cross-genre invariant

Every genre punishes the same thing: text that could be pasted
into any context unchanged. Anchor to the specifics of the actual
situation: the named person, the real date, the thing that
happened, the point being answered.
