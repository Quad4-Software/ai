# Classic style rules

Condensed. Orwell, Zinsser, Strunk, and King cover what to cut.
Gopen covers how to arrange what remains. Stylometry covers whose
voice it should be.

## Orwell, Politics and the English Language (1946)

1. Never use a metaphor, simile or other figure of speech which
   you are used to seeing in print.
2. Never use a long word where a short one will do.
3. If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out.
4. Never use the passive where you can use the active.
5. Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word or a jargon word
   if you can think of an everyday English equivalent.
6. Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright
   barbarous.

Rule 1 is the most LLM-relevant: models run on dead metaphors
(navigate the landscape, delve into, a testament to).

## Strunk and White, The Elements of Style

- Omit needless words. A sentence should contain no unnecessary
  words for the same reason a drawing has no unnecessary lines.

## Zinsser, On Writing Well

- Clutter is the disease of American writing. Audit every
  sentence: does each word do new work?
- Clear writing is clear thinking made visible.

## Gopen and Swan, The Science of Scientific Writing (1990)

Reader-expectation mechanics, the generative half:

- Stress position: the end of a sentence carries emphasis. Put
  new and important information there.
- Topic position: the start of a sentence sets context. Put old,
  known information there.
- Keep subject and verb adjacent where possible.
- Each unit of discourse serves one function.

Old info forward, new info back is the single most human-feeling
rhythm fix.

## King, On Writing (2000)

- The road to hell is paved with adverbs. Kill "really", "very",
  and intensifiers that assert rather than show.
