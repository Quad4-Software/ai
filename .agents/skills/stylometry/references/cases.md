# Case history

## Resolved or corroborated

- Federalist Papers (Mosteller and Wallace 1964): disputed essays
  to Madison. Function-word frequencies. Foundational result.
- Primary Colors (1996): Foster identified Joe Klein via word
  usage and punctuation. Klein confirmed.
- Unabomber (1995-96): broke on human recognition, not software.
  David Kaczynski recognized phrases in the published manifesto.
  Fitzgerald's comparison and Shuy's profiling (Chicago-area
  spellings clew/wilfully, archaic vocabulary) built the warrant
  case.
- J.K. Rowling as Robert Galbraith (2013): Juola (JGAAP) and
  Millican independently ranked Rowling closest on generic
  features. She confirmed. Lesson: stylometry corroborated a
  leak, it did not originate it.

## Convergent but unconfirmed

- Elena Ferrante: multiple independent methods point to Domenico
  Starnone for the novels. Never confirmed.
- QAnon (OrphAnalytics, Cafiero and Camps, NYT 2022): two
  independent methods agree on two regimes. No confirmation.
  Closed set of six suspects is a real limit.
- Satoshi Nakamoto: dozens of contradictory published
  attributions. Canonical example of open-set failure and
  motivated analysis. Cite only as a warning.

## Operational use

- BKA (Germany): Autorenerkennung unit since ~1990, KISTE expert
  system, national corpus of incriminating texts.
- Sockpuppet detection: Solorio, Hasan, Mizan (LREC 2014) built
  the first real-world corpus from Wikipedia sockpuppet
  investigations. Voting over short comments works around the
  short-text problem.
- Asylum: Juola reports stylometry supporting an asylum claim by
  linking an anonymous dissident's articles to him.

## Failures and limits

- Foster/Hatfill anthrax misidentification (settled suit, 2007).
- Foster's Funeral Elegy attribution retracted (2002).
- Both: pattern-match overconfidence plus motivated reasoning.
  The method inherits the analyst's candidate list.
